# The Lanterns in the Rain: Rain Path Benchmark Brief

## Status

**Draft benchmark scene brief. No generated image is approved or registered for runtime use.**

This brief defines the first production-art review target for Pixel Pals. It covers the opening environment of **The Lanterns in the Rain**, variant `rain-path`, in landscape orientation.

The resulting artwork is an environment layer. It must not contain a fixed player Pal, Fern, or any other foreground character. The game composites the child’s actual first Pal and the canonical first neighbor above the environment at runtime.

## Asset identity

- Adventure package: `lanterns-in-the-rain-adventure`
- Scene ID: `adventure-lanterns-in-the-rain`
- Variant: `rain-path`
- Review view: `rain-path-landscape`
- Intended output: WebP
- Target dimensions: 1600 × 1000
- Maximum compressed size: 1,100,000 bytes
- Current target status: concept draft
- Runtime eligibility: none until explicit approval and manifest promotion

## Story moment

A gentle shower has changed Willowmere’s familiar path. One handmade lantern has tipped into wet grass, another is partly hidden behind broad leaves, and a third remains upright farther along the curve. The scene should invite observation and cooperation. It must feel like a small caring problem, not danger, disaster, or a timed emergency.

The visual question is:

> What should the friends notice first?

## Emotional purpose

The scene should communicate:

- calm concern;
- curiosity;
- a familiar place made temporarily different;
- the possibility of helping together;
- warmth beginning to emerge inside cool rain;
- a village that feels inhabited rather than decorated.

Avoid peril, fear, dramatic storm lighting, damaged homes, distressed residents, urgency markers, reward effects, or a heroic rescue composition.

## Environment composition

Required environmental elements:

- a curved Willowmere footpath leading deeper into the village;
- three handmade lanterns with clearly different physical states;
- wet layered foliage and broad leaves;
- soft rain visible against darker leaves and distant surfaces;
- low mist or rain haze creating depth;
- glimpses of irregular Willowmere homes or porches in the middle distance;
- natural repairs, worn path edges, or small signs of everyday use;
- warm lantern glow that remains subordinate to the overall daylight scene;
- an open lower foreground reserved for live Pal overlays.

The three lantern states must remain understandable without relying on color:

1. tipped sideways in grass;
2. upright but partly concealed by leaves;
3. upright and visible farther along the path.

## Character-safe composition

No characters may be painted into the environment.

The lower foreground must preserve two readable character zones:

- left zone for the child-selected first Pal;
- right zone for Fern;
- clear silhouette separation between both zones;
- no lantern, branch, path sign, or high-contrast object directly behind either face area;
- no critical story information hidden beneath the live overlays.

Landscape safe-area target:

- left inset: 4 percent;
- right inset: 4 percent;
- bottom inset: 1 percent;
- character overlay height: approximately 66 percent of the scene.

The environment must still read coherently when either live Pal is Fox, Deer, Rabbit, Kitten, or Duckling.

## Visual language

Target qualities:

- warm painterly storybook illustration;
- gouache, watercolor, or softly textured digital-paint character;
- controlled natural edges rather than hard vector geometry;
- soft overcast illumination with gentle warm lantern accents;
- rounded natural forms and curved path language;
- handmade wood, paper, fabric, and garden materials;
- believable wet surfaces without photographic realism;
- calm depth with foreground, middle ground, and distant village layers;
- enough simplified shape clarity to read at 800 × 600.

The image must not imitate a named artist, studio, film, franchise, or copyrighted character style.

## Color and contrast

Suggested palette direction:

- rain blue-gray and muted teal in the sky and haze;
- layered Willowmere greens with warm earth beneath;
- soft cream, clay, and weathered wood in homes and path details;
- restrained honey-gold lantern light;
- one or two berry or flower accents at most.

Lantern meaning cannot depend on gold, green, or berry color choices. Position, angle, visibility, and silhouette carry the story first.

## Accessibility and reduced motion

The static frame must preserve all story meaning. Rain may later receive optional restrained motion, but no interaction clue may depend on animation.

The composition must remain understandable with:

- reduced motion;
- stronger contrast;
- larger interface text around the image;
- common forms of color-vision difference;
- both landscape and later portrait crops.

## Negative constraints

Do not include:

- characters or animal silhouettes in the foreground;
- text, signage, labels, logos, or UI;
- timers, counters, badges, sparkles, confetti, or reward effects;
- fantasy combat, weapons, danger, lightning, or violent weather;
- photorealism;
- generic flat vector art;
- dense decorative clutter;
- perfectly symmetrical buildings or garden beds;
- glowing magical symbols;
- a fixed Rabbit starter or any other implied canonical starter.

## Review tests

The draft fails review when:

- either live character zone is visually obstructed;
- the three lantern states are not distinguishable in grayscale;
- the scene appears dangerous rather than gently disrupted;
- the path does not guide attention deeper into Willowmere;
- important content disappears at 800 × 600;
- the image feels like a generic fantasy forest rather than a lived-in village;
- painterly texture becomes visual noise at tablet size;
- the composition depends on a specific starter silhouette;
- the portrait crop cannot be derived without losing the story.

## Promotion path

1. Generate a landscape concept draft.
2. Review composition, emotional tone, and live-character safe areas.
3. Revise until the benchmark direction is accepted.
4. Produce the paired portrait composition from the accepted scene language.
5. Compress and test both files against their budgets.
6. Complete provenance and rights review.
7. Promote the view to `benchmark`, then `approved`, and only later `production` through explicit manifest updates.

No stage is inferred from file existence alone.
