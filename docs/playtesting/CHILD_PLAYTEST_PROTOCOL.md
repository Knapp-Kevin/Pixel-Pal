# Pixel Pals Child Playtest Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol evaluates whether the first Pixel Pals experience is understandable, enjoyable, emotionally safe, and memorable without requiring adult coaching.

The purpose is not to prove that the design team was correct. It is to observe what the child actually does.

## Session Scope

The first structured playtest covers:

1. choosing and naming a first Pal;
2. completing one shared activity with Fern;
3. reading or recognizing the resulting memory;
4. completing the Great Tree friendship ceremony;
5. visiting the first Pal's home;
6. choosing one personal home object;
7. opening the memory journal;
8. closing and reopening the application.

## Adult Preparation

Before the child begins:

- use a test tablet or browser profile;
- confirm that no previous test world remains unless continuing a return-session test;
- confirm that sound, brightness, text size, and orientation are comfortable;
- prepare a world export before resetting an existing save;
- do not preselect or recommend a starter species;
- do not explain hidden systems unless the child is completely blocked.

## Observation Rules

The observer SHOULD:

- sit nearby without directing every choice;
- record actions and exact comments;
- note pauses, repeated taps, backtracking, laughter, frustration, and requests for help;
- distinguish a navigation problem from a reading problem;
- allow silence;
- let the child stop at any time.

The observer MUST NOT:

- praise a specific starter choice over another;
- ask leading questions such as "Do you love your Pal?";
- explain what the child is supposed to feel;
- turn the session into a performance test;
- pressure the child to complete the whole flow;
- use loss, rewards, or adult approval to keep the child playing.

## Prompts Allowed

Use only neutral prompts when needed:

- "What do you think you can do here?"
- "What were you trying to make happen?"
- "What do you think changed?"
- "Where would you look for that?"
- "You can stop whenever you are ready."

Do not provide the answer inside the question.

## What to Record

Record at minimum:

- which starter the child chose and why, if volunteered;
- whether naming felt easy or difficult;
- where the child first hesitated;
- whether the first shared activity felt like a meaningful choice;
- whether the child noticed Fern's relationship change;
- whether the child understood the Great Tree ceremony;
- whether the child noticed the persistent village marker;
- which home object the child chose;
- whether the journal felt like a story or a results screen;
- what the child repeated without prompting;
- what the child misunderstood;
- whether the child voluntarily wanted to continue.

## Return-Session Check

At least one follow-up should occur after a natural break, preferably the next day.

Before reopening the game, ask only:

- "What do you remember about the world?"
- "Who lived there?"
- "What happened between them?"
- "What do you think will still be there?"

Then reopen the application and observe whether the restored world matches the child's expectations.

## Success Signals

The vertical slice is succeeding when most children can:

- intentionally choose among all five starter options;
- recognize their chosen Pal after customization;
- understand that Fern is another person rather than an unlockable object;
- explain at least one discovered preference;
- recognize that the friendship changed something in Willowmere;
- find the home and journal without repeated adult instruction;
- remember a Pal, relationship, place, or event after a break;
- leave without fear of losing progress;
- express interest in another activity or another day in the world.

## Failure Signals

Treat these as design evidence, not child failure:

- repeated tapping without understanding;
- choosing only because an adult implied a preferred answer;
- inability to tell the starter options apart;
- believing the first Pal is the player's property;
- interpreting the journal as a score or checklist;
- not noticing the village or Great Tree change;
- anxiety about closing the application;
- accidental reset or destructive action;
- losing the saved world;
- boredom before the first meaningful interaction.

## Data and Privacy

- Do not collect a child's full name, voice, face, account identity, or contact information for routine internal playtesting.
- Use a session code or locally chosen nickname when notes need a reference.
- Keep world exports and observer notes local unless a parent or guardian has explicitly approved transfer.
- Delete test materials when they are no longer needed.
- Do not place child observation notes inside the game world or child-facing journal.

## Backup and Restore

The parent-facing tools can export and restore validated Pixel Pals world files.

Before importing a backup:

1. confirm that the file came from a trusted local playtest source;
2. export the current world when it still matters;
3. select the JSON backup through the parent-facing tools;
4. allow schema validation and migration to finish;
5. confirm that the application reopens the expected Pal, memories, home, and village state.

A valid imported world becomes the active checkpoint. The previously active world becomes the recovery checkpoint. An invalid file MUST leave the current world unchanged.

## Reset Procedure

The parent-facing tools require the adult to type `RESET` before local checkpoints are removed.

Before resetting:

1. export the world backup;
2. export observation notes;
3. confirm that the playtest is complete;
4. verify that the child understands this test world will be removed, when age-appropriate;
5. perform the reset away from the normal child-facing navigation.

## Review After Each Session

Classify findings as:

- **Blocking:** child cannot continue without intervention;
- **Confusing:** child continues but misunderstands the system;
- **Flat:** child understands but shows little interest;
- **Delight:** child responds positively without prompting;
- **Repeatable:** child chooses to do or revisit something again;
- **Memorable:** child recalls it after a break;
- **Safety concern:** experience creates anxiety, coercion, accidental loss, or privacy risk.

Fix blocking and safety concerns before increasing content volume.
