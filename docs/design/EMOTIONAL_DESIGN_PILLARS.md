# Pixel Pals Emotional Design Pillars

## This document exists to protect the heart of Pixel Pals.

Technology, mechanics, and content will evolve. These principles should not.

## Small Moments Become Lifelong Memories

Pixel Pals is not about saving the world.

It is about living in one.

The moments players remember should be ordinary:

- stacking stones with a friend;
- painting glowing symbols on a beach at sunset;
- climbing the tallest tree in the village;
- sharing a meal;
- helping repair a neighbor's home;
- watching the stars with an elder.

Relationships give these moments meaning.

## Fun Comes First

Pixel Pals is not an educational game disguised as entertainment.

It is a joyful game that naturally teaches empathy, cooperation, diversity, stewardship, curiosity, and community.

If a mechanic forces a choice between being educational and being genuinely enjoyable, the enjoyable experience wins.

Learning should be the souvenir, not the destination.

## The World Comes Before the Player

The player arrives in a world that already feels alive.

Villages have traditions.
Residents have histories.
Families already exist.
Great Trees have witnessed generations.

The player becomes part of the story rather than the center of it.

## Pals Are People

Never think of Pals as units or NPCs.

They are neighbors, friends, mentors, artists, builders, explorers, parents, children, and elders.

A memorable Pal is remembered for who they are, not for their statistics.

## Two Visual Layers

### The Storybook

Painterly illustrations communicate emotion, memory, lore, introductions, journals, loading scenes, and major story moments.

### The Living World

Gameplay uses simplified, readable, modular characters derived from the storybook references.

The illustrations define the canon.
The game models bring the world to life.

## Species Are Foundations, Characters Are Individuals

Species define biological identity.

Characters define personal identity.

Culture, clothing, memories, personality, relationships, and experiences make individuals memorable.

## Culture Is Not Biology

Environmental regions influence traditions, architecture, clothing, celebrations, art, music, food, and worldview.

Species are not reduced to stereotypes.

The same species can flourish in many different cultures.

## Great Trees Are Emotional Landmarks

Great Trees are where life's important moments are celebrated.

Friendships.
Families.
Adoptions.
Festivals.
Community gatherings.

Players associate trees with memories they created rather than rewards they collected.

## Every Village Should Answer One Question

What does it feel like to grow up here?

That answer should influence:

- games children invent;
- architecture;
- traditions;
- foods;
- festivals;
- music;
- daily routines;
- local stories;
- community values.

## Character Design Test

Every major character should answer:

- Who are they?
- What brings them joy?
- What worries them?
- What gift do they naturally possess?
- How do they hope to grow?
- How do they make their community better?

## World Design Test

Every new feature should strengthen at least one of:

- belonging;
- friendship;
- family;
- curiosity;
- wonder;
- community;
- joyful play.

If it does not, it probably belongs in a different game.
