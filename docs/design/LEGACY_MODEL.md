# Pixel Pals Legacy Model

## Core Principle

The first Pal begins the player's legacy, but does not contain it.

Every Pal the player meets, every friendship that forms, every family branch, every descendant, every mentor, every adopted child, every household, every shared tradition, and every remembered loss becomes part of the same living history.

Legacy is not a score, family pedigree, or collection percentage.

Legacy is the accumulated record of how lives touched one another and changed the world.

## Legacy Includes

A saved world's legacy MUST preserve meaningful connections involving:

- the player-named first Pal;
- later friends and companions;
- canon residents whose lives intersect with the player world;
- adopted, biological, blended, and chosen families;
- descendants across generations;
- mentors and learners;
- household members;
- visitors who become part of village history;
- Pals who move away;
- Pals who die;
- relationships that remain close;
- relationships that change, heal, or end.

No Pal becomes disposable because another Pal is newer, rarer, more capable, or descended from the first Pal.

## Legacy Graph

The conceptual legacy model is a graph, not a single bloodline.

Nodes may include:

- Pals;
- households;
- families;
- villages;
- Great Trees;
- memories;
- keepsakes;
- homes;
- traditions;
- skills;
- places;
- ceremonies.

Edges may include:

- friendship;
- companionship;
- parenthood;
- adoption;
- mentorship;
- shared household;
- shared adventure;
- teaching;
- gifting;
- remembrance;
- cultural exchange;
- migration;
- reconciliation.

Biological descent is one form of continuity, not the definition of legacy.

## Descendants

Descendants inherit more than appearance.

They may receive:

- genes and visible traits;
- family names or naming traditions;
- keepsakes;
- recipes;
- songs;
- stories;
- skills taught through generations;
- remembered places;
- household customs;
- relationships with family friends;
- consequences of earlier village decisions.

A descendant must still become an individual. They are not a replacement copy of an ancestor.

## Friends and Chosen Family

Friends are part of legacy even when no family relationship forms.

A lifelong friend may influence:

- later generations;
- household traditions;
- village architecture;
- a child's name;
- a learned skill;
- a memorial;
- a recurring festival;
- the meaning of a place.

The system MUST treat chosen family, adoption, and mentorship as first-class forms of continuity.

## Pals Who Leave

Moving away does not remove a Pal from history.

Their legacy may continue through:

- letters or stories;
- visits;
- objects left behind;
- relationships maintained at a distance;
- cultural practices they introduced;
- descendants or students;
- memories associated with shared places.

## Pals Who Die

Death ends new direct experiences with that Pal, but not their place in the world.

Their legacy may remain through:

- Great Tree remembrance;
- family and friendship records;
- inherited objects;
- stories;
- skills taught onward;
- jokes and sayings;
- visible changes they helped create;
- descendants;
- traditions;
- places that retain emotional meaning.

The world continues, and the Pal's influence continues within it.

## Player Experience

The player should be able to look backward through their world and understand:

- who was connected;
- what they experienced together;
- how families and friendships changed;
- where traditions came from;
- which choices shaped the village;
- how current Pals carry parts of earlier lives forward.

This history should be discoverable through stories and relationships, not presented as a sterile genealogy database.

## Great Trees and Legacy

Great Trees act as communal anchors for legacy.

They may preserve visible markers of:

- friendships;
- families;
- adoptions;
- mentorships;
- reconciliations;
- contributions;
- remembrance;
- major village transitions.

The Great Tree records communal recognition, while journals, homes, keepsakes, and relationships preserve personal meaning.

## Implementation Requirements

The simulation and save model MUST eventually support:

- stable Pal identities across the full save lifetime;
- relationship history rather than only current labels;
- parent, adoptive, guardian, and mentor links;
- memory references to Pals who are no longer active residents;
- immutable historical events with corrective annotations rather than destructive rewriting;
- family and friendship traversal across generations;
- Great Tree ceremony records;
- keepsake provenance;
- village changes linked to contributing Pals and memories.

Deleting an active Pal record MUST NOT be the normal implementation of moving away, aging, or death.

## Final Principle

The player's legacy begins with one named Pal and grows to include everyone whose life becomes connected to that beginning.

Every Pal matters.

Every friendship counts.

Every descendant adds a new branch without replacing the branches that came before.

The legacy is the world they made together.
